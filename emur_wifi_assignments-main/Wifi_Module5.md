# Module 5 – Modern Wi-Fi Standards & Roaming

### Question: What are the key features of Wi-Fi 6, 6E, and 7, and how do they differ from Wi-Fi 5?

Answer:

- Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac):
  - Works only in the 5 GHz band
  - Reaches speeds of around 3.5 Gbps
  - Supports MU-MIMO in the downlink direction
  - Uses channel widths up to 160 MHz

- Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax):
  - Supports both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz
  - Can reach around 9.6 Gbps
  - Introduces OFDMA for better multi-user sharing
  - Supports both uplink and downlink MU-MIMO
  - Performs better in crowded wireless environments

- Wi-Fi 6E:
  - Extends Wi-Fi 6 into the 6 GHz band
  - Opens up about 1200 MHz of additional spectrum
  - Reduces congestion and co-channel interference
  - Supports wider channels and higher practical throughput

- Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be):
  - Targets speeds up to about 30 Gbps
  - Supports 320 MHz channel width
  - Adds Multi-Link Operation
  - Improves OFDMA and MU-MIMO further
  - Uses more advanced modulation for better efficiency

---

### Question: What is the role of OFDMA in Wi-Fi 6?

Answer:
OFDMA stands for Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access. It divides a channel into smaller resource units and assigns those pieces to different users at the same time.

Benefits:
- Allows multiple devices to communicate simultaneously
- Lowers waiting time and latency
- Uses spectrum more efficiently
- Works especially well in dense environments

---

### Question: What is Target Wake Time (TWT) and how does it benefit IoT devices?

Answer:
Target Wake Time schedules when a device should wake up and talk to the access point. This helps devices avoid staying awake longer than necessary.

Benefits:
- Reduces power consumption
- Extends battery life for IoT devices
- Cuts down unnecessary transmissions
- Helps reduce congestion in the network

---

### Question: What is the significance of the 6 GHz band in Wi-Fi 6E?

Answer:
The 6 GHz band is important because it gives Wi-Fi access to a much cleaner and larger block of spectrum.

- It adds around 1200 MHz of new spectrum
- It offers channels with very little interference
- It supports wider bandwidths, including 160 MHz channels
- It improves performance in busy wireless environments

---

### Question: Compare Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 6E in terms of range, bandwidth, and interference.

Answer:

- Range:
  - Wi-Fi 6 covers 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, so range depends on which band is used
  - Wi-Fi 6E uses 6 GHz, which has shorter range because higher frequencies do not travel as far

- Bandwidth:
  - Wi-Fi 6E usually has higher bandwidth because it has more spectrum available

- Interference:
  - Wi-Fi 6E has much lower interference
  - Wi-Fi 6 may still face congestion in the older bands

---

### Question: What are the major innovations in Wi-Fi 7?

Answer:
Wi-Fi 7 brings major improvements in speed, latency, and flexibility.

- Very high speeds, up to about 30 Gbps
- Support for 320 MHz channels
- Multi-Link Operation for using more than one band at once
- Stronger OFDMA and MU-MIMO performance
- Lower latency for gaming, video calls, and real-time use

---

### Question: Explain Multi-Link Operation (MLO) and its impact.

Answer:
Multi-Link Operation lets a device use multiple bands or links at the same time, such as 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz.

Impact:
- Increases throughput by combining multiple links
- Reduces latency by shifting traffic away from busy channels
- Improves connection stability and reliability

---

### Question: What is the purpose of 802.11k and 802.11v in roaming?

Answer:

- 802.11k:
  - Gives the client information about nearby APs
  - Helps the device make a better roaming choice

- 802.11v:
  - Lets the network suggest a better AP
  - Improves the handoff decision process

Together, they help the client roam more smoothly.

---

### Question: What is 802.11r and why is it important?

Answer:
802.11r adds Fast BSS Transition, which reduces the delay when a device moves from one access point to another.

Benefits:
- Faster roaming
- Less interruption during movement
- Better support for voice, streaming, and similar real-time applications

---

### Question: How do 802.11k, 802.11v, and 802.11r work together?

Answer:
- 802.11k gives the client AP information
- 802.11v helps guide the client toward a better AP
- 802.11r speeds up the handoff itself

Together:
- They support seamless roaming
- They reduce packet loss and delay
- They improve continuity in enterprise wireless networks
