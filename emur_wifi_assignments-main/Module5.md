# Module 5 – Modern Wi-Fi Standards & Roaming

### Question: What are the key features of Wi-Fi 6, 6E, and 7, and how do they differ from Wi-Fi 5?

Answer:

- Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac):
  - Operates only in 5 GHz band  
  - Speeds up to ~3.5 Gbps  
  - Supports MU-MIMO (downlink only)  
  - Uses up to 160 MHz channels  

- Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax):
  - Supports 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands  
  - Speeds up to ~9.6 Gbps  
  - Introduces OFDMA for multi-user efficiency  
  - Supports both uplink and downlink MU-MIMO  
  - Better performance in dense environments  

- Wi-Fi 6E:
  - Extends Wi-Fi 6 into 6 GHz band  
  - Adds ~1200 MHz of new spectrum  
  - Reduces congestion and interference  
  - Supports wider channels and higher throughput  

- Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be):
  - Speeds up to ~30 Gbps  
  - Supports 320 MHz channel width  
  - Introduces Multi-Link Operation (MLO)  
  - Enhanced OFDMA and MU-MIMO  
  - Uses higher-order modulation for improved throughput  

---

### Question: What is the role of OFDMA in Wi-Fi 6?

Answer:
OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access) divides a channel into smaller subcarriers and assigns them to multiple users simultaneously.

Benefits:
- Multiple devices can transmit at the same time  
- Reduces latency and waiting time  
- Improves spectrum utilization  
- Ideal for high-density environments  

---

### Question: What is Target Wake Time (TWT) and how does it benefit IoT devices?

Answer:
TWT schedules when a device should wake up and communicate with the access point.

Benefits:
- Reduces power consumption  
- Extends battery life for IoT devices  
- Minimizes unnecessary communication  
- Decreases network congestion  

---

### Question: What is the significance of the 6 GHz band in Wi-Fi 6E?

Answer:
- Provides additional ~1200 MHz spectrum  
- Offers cleaner channels with minimal interference  
- Supports wider bandwidth (up to 160 MHz)  
- Improves performance in crowded environments  

---

### Question: Compare Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 6E in terms of range, bandwidth, and interference.

Answer:

- Range:
  - Similar in 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz  
  - 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E) has shorter range due to higher frequency  

- Bandwidth:
  - Wi-Fi 6E offers higher bandwidth due to additional spectrum  

- Interference:
  - Wi-Fi 6E experiences lower interference  
  - Wi-Fi 6 may face congestion in existing bands  

---

### Question: What are the major innovations in Wi-Fi 7?

Answer:
- Extremely high speeds (up to ~30 Gbps)  
- 320 MHz channel support  
- Multi-Link Operation (MLO)  
- Enhanced MU-MIMO and OFDMA  
- Ultra-low latency for real-time applications  

---

### Question: Explain Multi-Link Operation (MLO) and its impact.

Answer:
MLO allows devices to use multiple frequency bands simultaneously (2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, 6 GHz).

Impact:
- Increases throughput by combining bandwidth  
- Reduces latency by avoiding congested channels  
- Provides more stable and reliable connections  

---

### Question: What is the purpose of 802.11k and 802.11v in roaming?

Answer:

- 802.11k:
  - Provides information about nearby APs  
  - Helps devices choose the best connection  

- 802.11v:
  - Allows network to guide client decisions  
  - Improves handoff efficiency  

Both contribute to smoother roaming in wireless networks.

---

### Question: What is 802.11r and why is it important?

Answer:
802.11r enables Fast BSS Transition, reducing the delay when switching between access points.

Benefits:
- Faster roaming  
- Minimal connection disruption  
- Essential for real-time applications like VoIP and streaming  

---

### Question: How do 802.11k, 802.11v, and 802.11r work together?

Answer:
- 802.11k: Provides AP information for decision-making  
- 802.11v: Guides device to the optimal AP  
- 802.11r: Speeds up the handoff process  

Together:
- Enable seamless roaming  
- Reduce latency and packet loss  
- Ensure uninterrupted connectivity in enterprise networks
